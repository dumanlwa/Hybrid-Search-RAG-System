# Northstar Financial Services, Inc. - FY2025 Annual Report

## Company overview

Northstar Financial Services, Inc. is a fictional diversified financial services company used for this retrieval demonstration. Northstar provides retail banking, commercial lending, wealth management, and payment services to customers in the United States. The company operates through three reportable segments: Consumer Banking, Commercial Finance, and Wealth and Payments.

Northstar's strategy is to build durable customer relationships while maintaining disciplined underwriting, strong liquidity, and conservative capital levels. The company invests in digital account opening, fraud prevention, data security, and tools that help customers manage cash flow. Management evaluates growth opportunities against risk-adjusted returns rather than loan or account growth alone.

## Selected financial highlights

For the year ended December 31, 2025, Northstar reported total revenue of $8.42 billion, compared with $7.96 billion in 2024. Net income was $1.31 billion, or $4.36 per diluted share, compared with net income of $1.18 billion, or $3.91 per diluted share, in the prior year. The increase in net income reflected higher fee income and disciplined operating expense management, partially offset by higher provision expense.

Average earning assets were $112.7 billion in 2025, compared with $106.9 billion in 2024. The average yield on earning assets increased as new loans repriced at higher rates. Funding costs also increased because customers moved balances into higher-yielding deposits and because the company used wholesale funding to support seasonal liquidity needs.

Return on average common equity was 13.8% in 2025, compared with 13.1% in 2024. The efficiency ratio was 54.7%, compared with 55.9% in 2024. These ratios are management measures used to monitor profitability and operating performance; they should be considered alongside GAAP results and are not necessarily comparable with similarly named measures used by other companies.

## Consumer Banking segment

Consumer Banking provides checking and savings accounts, residential mortgages, home equity loans, credit cards, and personal loans. The segment had 2.4 million active households at year-end 2025, an increase of 6% from the prior year. Digital channels originated 71% of new consumer accounts, while branches remained important for complex lending and advice.

Consumer deposits averaged $54.2 billion during 2025. Checking balances grew 4%, but savings balances were mixed as customers responded to higher market rates. The segment's net interest margin declined modestly because deposit pricing increased faster than the yield on some fixed-rate assets. Management expects margin performance to remain sensitive to customer rate behavior and the timing of central bank policy changes.

Consumer loan balances were $31.6 billion at December 31, 2025. Residential mortgage balances declined 3% as repayments and refinancing activity exceeded new production. Credit card balances increased 9%, supported by account growth and higher spending volume. The allowance for consumer credit losses increased during the year because of portfolio growth and a modest deterioration in early-stage delinquencies.

## Commercial Finance segment

Commercial Finance provides loans and treasury services to middle-market businesses, commercial real estate borrowers, and public-sector organizations. Commercial loans were $42.8 billion at year-end, compared with $39.7 billion at the end of 2024. Growth was concentrated in asset-based lending, equipment finance, and working-capital facilities for companies with recurring cash flow.

The segment recorded $74 million of net charge-offs in 2025, compared with $51 million in 2024. Net charge-offs remained below the company's long-term average, but management increased its monitoring of office properties, leveraged borrowers, and industries affected by lower consumer demand. New commercial commitments are subject to borrower-level risk ratings, collateral analysis, covenant review, and stress testing.

Commercial real estate exposure was $9.6 billion, including $3.1 billion secured by office properties. The weighted-average loan-to-value ratio for the office portfolio was 57%. Approximately 18% of office loans mature in 2026, and the company has begun renewal discussions with borrowers before contractual maturity. Management considers property cash flow, occupancy, sponsor support, and updated appraisals when determining credit reserves.

## Wealth and Payments segment

Wealth and Payments provides investment advice, custody, merchant acquiring, card processing, and digital payment services. Assets under management were $78.4 billion at year-end 2025, up from $69.2 billion in 2024. The increase reflected market appreciation, net client inflows, and higher adoption of managed portfolios.

Payment volume increased 11% to $186.5 billion. Payment revenue grew faster than volume because of expanded fraud-management services and improved pricing for larger merchants. The segment continues to invest in authorization reliability, tokenization, and transaction monitoring. A disruption in payment processing could lead to lost revenue, customer remediation costs, regulatory scrutiny, and reputational damage.

## Interest-rate and market risk

Northstar is exposed to changes in interest rates because assets and liabilities reprice at different times and because customers can change the mix of products they use. The asset-liability committee monitors duration, deposit behavior, funding concentration, and modeled net interest income under multiple rate scenarios.

At December 31, 2025, the company estimated that an immediate 100-basis-point increase in short-term interest rates would reduce projected twelve-month net interest income by approximately 1.4%, assuming no balance-sheet growth and stable customer behavior. An immediate 100-basis-point decrease would increase projected net interest income by approximately 0.8%. These estimates are model-based and actual results may differ materially.

The securities portfolio contains U.S. government obligations, agency mortgage-backed securities, municipal bonds, and corporate debt. Unrealized losses can result from changes in market rates even when management does not expect credit losses. The treasury team reviews liquidity needs before selling securities because a sale may create a realized loss and change the composition of the investment portfolio.

## Credit risk and allowance methodology

Credit risk is the risk that a borrower or counterparty will fail to meet contractual obligations. Northstar estimates expected credit losses using historical loss information, current portfolio characteristics, and reasonable and supportable forecasts. The allowance model includes assumptions about unemployment, gross domestic product, home prices, commercial property values, and borrower payment behavior.

Management reviews model output using qualitative adjustments when historical data does not fully reflect current conditions. Examples include changes in underwriting standards, portfolio concentrations, disaster exposure, payment deferrals, and emerging industry risks. The allowance for credit losses was $1.24 billion at December 31, 2025, compared with $1.10 billion at December 31, 2024.

Loans are placed on nonaccrual status when the collection of principal or interest is considered doubtful or when payments are materially past due under company policy. Interest income is generally recognized on a cash basis for nonaccrual loans only after the recorded investment has been recovered. Troubled debt restructurings and other modified loans are evaluated for expected losses using updated repayment assumptions.

## Liquidity and capital

Liquidity is the ability to meet obligations when due without unacceptable losses. Northstar maintains cash, highly liquid securities, secured borrowing capacity, and diversified deposits as sources of liquidity. The liquidity risk committee reviews stress scenarios involving deposit outflows, collateral haircuts, market disruption, and reduced access to unsecured funding.

The company had $9.8 billion of cash and cash equivalents at year-end 2025. Available borrowing capacity from the Federal Home Loan Bank and the Federal Reserve discount window totaled $18.6 billion, subject to collateral and operational requirements. Management does not treat borrowing capacity as a substitute for stable core deposits.

Northstar's common equity tier 1 capital ratio was 12.4%, compared with a regulatory minimum, including buffers, of 7.0%. The total risk-based capital ratio was 15.1%. Capital planning considers expected earnings, balance-sheet growth, stress losses, dividends, share repurchases, and potential changes in regulatory requirements.

The board declared quarterly dividends totaling $0.92 per common share during 2025. Future dividends and repurchases are subject to board approval, regulatory expectations, earnings, capital levels, and the company's assessment of economic conditions.

## Operational and technology risk

Northstar relies on information technology, cloud providers, telecommunications networks, payment networks, and third-party service providers. A cyberattack, software defect, prolonged outage, or failure of a critical vendor could interrupt customer services and expose confidential information. The company maintains incident response plans, backup systems, security monitoring, employee training, and vendor oversight programs.

Cybersecurity spending was $146 million in 2025, including investments in identity protection, privileged-access management, endpoint detection, and security operations personnel. No cybersecurity incident during 2025 had a material adverse effect on the company's financial condition. However, the company cannot guarantee that future incidents will not have a material effect.

Operational risk losses were $38 million in 2025. The largest categories were payment errors, third-party service interruptions, and customer remediation. Management uses risk and control self-assessments, internal audit testing, key risk indicators, and scenario analysis to identify and reduce operational risk.

## Regulation and compliance

Northstar is subject to extensive regulation covering capital, liquidity, consumer protection, privacy, fair lending, anti-money laundering, sanctions, cybersecurity, and resolution planning. Regulatory requirements can change the cost of products, limit business activities, require additional capital, or increase compliance expenses.

The company maintains a three-lines-of-defense model. Business teams own day-to-day risks and controls. Independent risk management establishes policies, challenges risk assessments, and monitors compliance. Internal audit provides independent assurance to the audit committee and the board.

During 2025, Northstar paid $21 million in regulatory penalties and customer remediation related to historical account-disclosure errors. The company completed corrective actions required under the related consent order and continues to monitor compliance through periodic testing. Future examinations could identify additional issues or require further remediation.

## Accounting policies

The consolidated financial statements are prepared in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States. Preparation requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect reported assets, liabilities, revenue, expenses, and related disclosures.

The most significant estimates relate to the allowance for credit losses, fair values of financial instruments, goodwill impairment, deferred tax assets, and the fair value of servicing rights. Changes in economic forecasts, interest rates, borrower behavior, or market liquidity may cause actual results to differ from these estimates.

Goodwill was $2.7 billion at December 31, 2025. Management performed its annual impairment assessment and concluded that the estimated fair value of each reporting unit exceeded its carrying value. A sustained decline in market capitalization, adverse changes in business performance, or a significant increase in the cost of capital could require a future impairment charge.
